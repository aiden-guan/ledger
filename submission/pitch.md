# LedgerLens

**Find the leak. Show the proof. Keep the owner in control.**

## Short project description

LedgerLens is a financial investigator for small businesses. It connects the books to specific findings—duplicate payments, rising costs, margin erosion, and underpricing—then shows the records behind each claim and prepares a fix for the owner to review.

The fictional Acme Outdoor demo identifies $4,286 in first-year opportunity from 1,187 bank and sales records: $1,058 one-time and $3,228 recurring annually. These are computed opportunities, not recovered funds. The full product combines deterministic finance calculations with evidence-grounded explanations, payment screening, a forecast, and an audit trail.

For the Figma track, we reconstructed and refined the evidence-to-action flow in native Figma Design, exported its semantic variables into a runnable prototype, and documented the actual tool choices and friction in Figma Slides. Earlier Figma files were lost, so the submission clearly distinguishes the current reconstruction from historical work.

## Thirty-second opening

Small businesses lose money in details that are easy to miss: an invoice paid twice, a supplier increase, a price that no longer covers the margin. LedgerLens finds the leak and shows the proof. Here, two payments share one invoice. The system prepares a refund request, but the owner decides what happens next. Our Figma workflow makes that journey inspectable—from the first finding to the final decision.

## Claims to preserve during judging

- Fictional demo records and fixture market evidence are labeled.
- Identified opportunity is distinct from recovered cash.
- The focused Evidence Lab records local demo approval; it never sends email or moves money.
- Code generation was performed in Codex using the Figma flow and exported variables. Make import was attempted but blocked before import.
- The video is an edited narrated Figma prototype walkthrough, not a live browser recording.
- Original lost Figma work is reported history; reconstructed files are current artifacts.
